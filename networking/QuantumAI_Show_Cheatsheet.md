## WHAT TO TALK ABOUT WITH LEADERS (topics + openers)
Pick the opener that matches their role. Keep it curious, not salesy.

**To a Head of Data / Data Science leader (Escayola, Ahuja):**
- "What's been the hardest part of actually getting AI/analytics adopted in your org — the tech or the people side?"
- "You mentioned [X] in your talk — how do you decide which data projects are worth doing vs noise?"
- "For someone early in their data career, what skills do you think will matter most in the next few years?"

**To an AI governance person (Dimireva):**
- "I did some independent research on AI governance and surveillance risk, so your session really resonated. How does governance actually work day-to-day inside a company like Arup?"
- "Where do you see the biggest gap between AI governance in theory vs in practice?"

**To an AI engineer (Konda):**
- "What does 'applied AI' actually look like day-to-day in your team vs how it's talked about at events like this?"
- "What do you wish more junior people understood before joining an AI team?"

**To a talent/ecosystem person (Kalmykova):**
- "I'm trying to break into data/AI as a 2027 grad — what are you seeing work for people in my position right now?"

**Universal safe questions (work with anyone):**
- "What brought you to this event / what are you hoping to get out of today?"
- "What's one thing about AI adoption that people outside the field get wrong?"
- "How did you get into this area yourself?"

## HOW TO APPROACH (the mechanics)
- Easiest moment: right AFTER their session, when they step off stage or stand near it.
- Or at the coffee/food breaks — people are relaxed and open.
- Walk up, smile, "Hi, I really enjoyed your session" + one specific thing they said.
- If they're mid-conversation, wait, hover nearby, join when there's a pause.
- Groups are EASIER than one-on-one — stand at the edge, nod, add a short comment, you're in.
- Keep it to 2-3 minutes. Don't monopolise. End with: "Would it be alright to connect on LinkedIn?"

## DEALING WITH NERVES
- Everyone there is also there to network — approaching them is welcome, not rude.
- Going solo is an ADVANTAGE: no friend-huddle, you meet more people.
- You don't need to impress. Be curious and ask good questions — people love talking about their work.
- Your goal per person is tiny: one good chat + a LinkedIn connection. That's a win.
